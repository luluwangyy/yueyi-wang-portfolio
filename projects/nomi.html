Nomi

Overview

Nomi is a two-sided medication-tracking app with shared live state, helping parents log what they’ve taken while giving family members a lightweight way to check in when something hasn’t been confirmed.

My Role

I took Nomi from early research through a working prototype:

Research
Family interviews · problem framing · user flows
Design

Build
React / TypeScript · shared live state · responsive implementation
Iteration
Usability testing · interaction refinement · live prototype

Project Context

I started with a simple assumption: older adults needed better medication reminders.

But after speaking with 4 families, I realized that wasn’t quite the problem.

Parents often remembered to take their medication. What they forgot was whether they had already taken it, especially when their usual routine changed.

At the same time, existing pill boxes and reminder systems mostly served the person taking the medication. Family members who cared about them had no simple way to know what was going on without calling.

The problem wasn’t just forgetting. It was uncertainty on both sides.

For parents

“Did I already take this?”

For family

“Is everything okay?”

That shifted Nomi from a reminder app into a two-sided experience built around self-confirmation for parents and lightweight reassurance for family members.

Final Artifact

Nomi connects two sides of the same medication routine.

01

Parent

Parents see today’s medications and mark them as taken when they actually take them.

02

Family

The family view reflects the parent’s confirmations in real time, and can edit medication schedules for parents.

03

Gentle Check-In

Parent confirms → family knows → family can check in when needed.

Key Design Moments

01

Designing for two generations

Nomi needed to work for two very different users: parents in their 50s–60s managing medication, and adult children in their 20s–30s checking in from a distance.

For the parent experience, I focused on two accessibility decisions:

01 Simpler language

Reduce unnecessary captions and replace unfamiliar terms with short, direct language.

02 Adjustable text size

Parents can choose the font size they feel comfortable with.

Instead of a fixed accessibility mode, the layout adapts as text grows, keeping medication names, status, and actions readable without breaking the interface.

Accessibility shaped both what people read and how they read it.

02

Creating something between silence and a phone call

For family members, the problem was almost the reverse.

When something wasn’t confirmed, there were usually two choices:

Option 01

Say nothing and keep wondering

Option 02

Call

But sometimes a phone call felt like too much for such a small question.

A phone call felt like too much. Silence felt like not enough.

That led to Gentle Check-In.

Instead of sending an alarming notification, a family member can send a small check-in through Nomi. The parent can respond directly, resolving the uncertainty without making the interaction feel urgent.

This became important to the tone of the whole product: Nomi should help family members stay connected without making every missing confirmation feel like a problem.

Outcome

I developed Nomi into a working two-sided prototype rather than stopping at static Figma screens.

The final experience connects the parent and family views through shared live state, so an action on one side immediately changes what the other person sees.

4 families interviewed research changed the product direction
Parent ↔ Family working two-sided live prototype
Gentle Check-In a new interaction for resolving uncertainty
Adjustable Type accessibility built into the parent experience

One action on either side gives the other person clearer, calmer information.

Reflection

Nomi made me think differently about what “helpful” means in a family-care product.

More reminders, more alerts, and more visibility aren’t automatically better.

Helping someone doesn’t always mean watching more closely. Sometimes it means giving both sides just enough information.
